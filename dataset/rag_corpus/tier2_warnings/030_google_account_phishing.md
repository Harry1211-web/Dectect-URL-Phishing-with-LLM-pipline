# Avoid and report phishing emails — Google account phishing (Google)

Phishing uses emails, messages, ads or sites that look like ones you already use to steal passwords
or break into accounts. Fake Google sign-in pages and fake Google security alerts are among the
most common.

## Google never asks for your password by email

If you are signed in, emails from Google never ask you to enter the password of that account. Do not
enter your Gmail or Google Account password on a page reached through a link in a message; go
directly to the website instead.

## Fake security alerts

To check a security email that seems to come from Google, open myaccount.google.com/notifications
directly instead of clicking the message. Be wary of urgent or too-good-to-be-true messages.

## Never share private information

Do not answer requests for usernames, passwords, ID numbers, bank or card numbers, PINs or
birthdays by email, text or phone.

## Checking links

Hover over a link before clicking: if the address does not match the link's description, it may be a
phishing site. Chrome Safe Browsing and Password Alert warn when a Google password is typed on a
non-Google site.
