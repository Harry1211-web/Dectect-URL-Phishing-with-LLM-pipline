# How To Recognize and Avoid Phishing Scams (FTC)

Phishing emails and texts try to steal passwords, account numbers or identity numbers. They often
pretend to come from a bank, credit card company, utility, online payment service or a well-known
subscription brand such as Netflix.

## Common phishing stories

The message reports suspicious activity or a login attempt that never happened, claims a problem
with the account or payment information, asks to confirm personal or financial information,
includes an invoice you do not recognise, links to a payment page, says you are eligible for a
government refund, or offers a coupon for free things.

## Signs in a typical example

A generic greeting, a claim that the account is on hold because of a billing problem, and a link to
"update your payment details". Legitimate companies do not send a link by email or text to update
payment information.

## What to do

Ask: do I have an account with this company or know this person? If not, it is probably phishing.
If yes, contact the company with a phone number or website you know is real, not the link or number
in the message. Use multi-factor authentication and keep software updated.

## How to report

Forward phishing emails to reportphishing@apwg.org, forward phishing texts to 7726 (SPAM), and
report to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
