# Phishing campaigns targeting crypto hardware wallet users (Ledger)

Fake wallet websites, apps, emails, letters and calls all aim at one thing: the 24-word recovery
phrase (seed phrase). Anyone who gets it can empty the wallet. A real wallet maker never asks for
the recovery phrase; it is only ever typed on the hardware device itself, never on a website or
computer.

## Fake wallet apps and websites

Look-alike apps or sites show a fake error or security alert and ask the user to "verify" by
entering the recovery phrase, with buttons such as "Verify Now", "Secure My Account Now" or "Claim
your protection device". Download wallet software only from the maker's official domain.

## Fake emails, letters and texts

Messages styled as data-breach notices, surveys, firmware or 2FA updates, KYC checks or "vault
address" letters ask the user to scan a QR code, visit a site or send funds to a new address.
Wallet makers do not contact customers by SMS, WhatsApp, Telegram, phone or postal letter, and never
block a device for KYC reasons.

## Fake support and social media

Impersonators of support staff or employees contact users on social media or by phone, often after
opening a fake support ticket, and claim the account is compromised. Official support never sends
direct messages.

## Scam tokens and airdrops

Unsolicited tokens or NFTs with prize wording lure users to "claim" sites that drain the wallet.

## How to verify

Check the sender domain carefully (look-alike spellings, long domains that only contain the brand
name), use only the official support site, and reject any transaction on the device that you did not
create.
