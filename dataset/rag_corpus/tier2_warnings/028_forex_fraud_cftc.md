# Forex fraud and eight things to know before trading forex (CFTC)

The CFTC sees a growing number of complaints from people who deposited money with unregistered
offshore forex dealers, found through social media, and then could not withdraw their money or
profits.

## Red flags of a forex scam

Promises of high or guaranteed returns, or that forex has no losing ("bear") market; contact through
social media, dating or messaging apps and pressure to move to WhatsApp or Telegram; dealers with no
registration, no verifiable address and no phone; deposits accepted only in bitcoin or other digital
assets; leverage above legal limits; requests to send money quickly.

## The trading platform belongs to the dealer

Over-the-counter forex apps and websites connect you to the dealer, not to an exchange. The dealer
controls the prices and trade results shown, and unregistered dealers have manipulated them. Two out
of three retail forex customers lose money.

## Withdrawals blocked, extra fees demanded

Deposits are not protected. Be wary of dealers that demand undisclosed commissions, invented taxes
or more deposits before a withdrawal: you should never have to pay more money to get your money back.

## Influencers and affiliates

Salespeople, influencers and affiliate marketers may be paid for every new customer without
disclosing it, and downplay the risks.

## Protective steps

Check the dealer's registration and disciplinary history (CFTC, NFA) before sending money, get all
information in writing, and never deposit more than you can afford to lose.
