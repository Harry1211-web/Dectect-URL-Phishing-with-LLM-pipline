# What To Know About Cryptocurrency and Scams (FTC)

Crypto payments usually cannot be reversed and are not insured, so scammers ask for them. Only
scammers demand payment in cryptocurrency: legitimate businesses and government agencies never ask
you to pay in crypto or to buy crypto to "protect" your money.

## Investment scams and fake trading platforms

An unexpected "investment manager" or online contact promises to grow your money if you buy crypto
and send it to their account or platform. The investment website looks real but is fake: the
dashboard shows profits, then withdrawals are blocked or need high fees. Warning signs: guaranteed
returns, "zero risk", quick profits, vague explanations of where the money goes.

## Giveaways, fake celebrities and fake tokens

Promises to double or multiply any crypto you send, often using a celebrity or company name, are
always fake. Scammers also impersonate companies launching their own coin or token with ads and
slick websites; real launches are widely reported in established media.

## Wallet and payment tricks

Being told to send crypto to a wallet address "for safe keeping", to scan a QR code that pays a
scammer's wallet, or to deposit cash in a crypto ATM are scam signs.

## Impersonation, romance and blackmail

Scammers pose as Amazon, Microsoft, FedEx, a bank or a government agency and claim fraud or a legal
problem that must be fixed by buying crypto. Dating contacts who ask for crypto or give investment
advice, and emails threatening to publish compromising material unless paid in crypto, are scams.

## What to do

Do not pay anyone who contacts you unexpectedly and demands crypto, do not click links in unexpected
messages, and do not call numbers from pop-ups. Search the company or coin name with "scam",
"review" or "complaint" before investing. Report to ReportFraud.ftc.gov, CFTC.gov/complaint,
sec.gov/tcr or ic3.gov.
