# How to spot a scam email, text message or call (NCSC UK)

Criminals contact people by email, text, phone or social media while posing as a trusted person or
organisation. Scams no longer always have obvious spelling mistakes, and scam emails increasingly
contain QR codes that lead to scam websites.

## Five signs of a scam message

- Authority: claims to come from an official source such as a bank, doctor, solicitor or government
  department.
- Urgency: a short deadline, or threats of fines or other consequences.
- Emotion: makes you panic, afraid, hopeful or curious.
- Scarcity: offers something in short supply (tickets, money, a cure).
- Current events: exploits news, big events or times of year such as tax season.

## How to check whether a message is genuine

Contact the organisation directly using details from its official website, not the number or link
in the message. Official sources never ask for personal information by email and never ask you to
confirm bank details over the phone. If a caller seems suspicious, hang up and call the organisation
on a number you trust.

## Reporting

The NCSC takes down scam emails and websites that are reported to it; by July 2026 it had received
more than 58 million reports, leading to the removal of 256,000 scams across 454,800 URLs.
