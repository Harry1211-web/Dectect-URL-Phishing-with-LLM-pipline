# How To Spot, Avoid, and Report Tech Support Scams

Tech support scammers convince people that their computer has a problem, to get remote access,
financial information or a payment for a fake fix.

## Fake pop-ups and pages with a phone number

A page or pop-up imitating a well-known company (Microsoft, Apple, an antivirus brand) says the
computer is infected or locked and urges the visitor to call a phone number. Real security warnings
never ask you to call a phone number. Scammers also buy search results and ads for "tech support"
so that people call their number instead of the real company.

## What happens on the call

The fake technician asks for remote access, pretends to scan the computer, "finds" malware and asks
for a fee. They push payment by gift card, wire transfer, bank transfer, cryptocurrency or payment
app, because these work like cash and are hard to reverse.

## Fake invoices and subscription renewals

Emails or texts claim the person was charged hundreds of dollars to renew a support subscription
(names such as Geek Squad, McAfee or Norton) and must call within 24 hours to dispute it. The
scammer then asks for remote access, sends the victim to a spoofed site to enter bank or card details
for a "refund", and claims an overpayment that must be paid back by gift card or crypto.

## Fake government helpers

Some callers say the accounts were hacked or linked to crimes, then pass the victim to a fake
government agent with a fake badge or case number who asks to move money to "protect" it, withdraw
cash or buy gold. Government employees never ask you to transfer money to protect it.

## How to avoid and report

Legitimate tech companies do not contact you by phone, email or text about a computer problem.
Contact a company only with a number you know is real, not the one in a pop-up or message. If you
gave a password, change it everywhere you reused it. Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
